Beam — EN 1992-1-1:2004
=======================

Provisions implemented for ``RectangularBeam`` when the section is built with
``Concrete_EN_1992_2004``.

Scope
-----

**Implemented**

- Flexure: design bending resistance of singly and doubly reinforced rectangular
  sections, required steel area, minimum reinforcement, ductility limit on the
  neutral axis.
- Shear: members without shear reinforcement (§6.2.2) and with shear reinforcement
  via the variable-strut-inclination method (§6.2.3), strut crushing limit, minimum
  stirrups and spacing limits.

**Not implemented**: torsion (§6.3), punching in the beam module (see
``PunchingSlab``), serviceability limit states — crack width (§7.3) and deflection
(§7.4) — anchorage and lap lengths (§8), fatigue (§6.8), and prestressing. The
:math:`A_p` term appears in the shear reinforcement ratio but is fixed at zero.

Nationally Determined Parameters
--------------------------------

EN 1992-1-1 leaves several values to the National Annex. mento uses the **recommended
values from the Eurocode text** for the parameters identified below.
The default :math:`\alpha_{cc}=0.85` is a separate Mento choice; the EN
recommended value is 1.0 (§3.1.6(1), Note). This is not a complete national
annex implementation:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 16 16 68

   * - Parameter
     - Value
     - Note
   * - :math:`\alpha_{cc}`
     - 0.85
     - The Eurocode recommends 1.0; mento follows the UK NA and CEB Model Code
       value of 0.85, which the *Designers' Guide to EN 1992-1-1* (p. 62) argues
       for on the grounds that 1.0 is unconservative for compression zones at
       failure. For shear, :math:`\alpha_{cc} = 1.0` is used instead.
   * - :math:`\gamma_c,\ \gamma_s`
     - 1.50, 1.15
     - Persistent and transient design situations
   * - :math:`\delta`
     - 0.85
     - Redistribution ratio, i.e. 15% moment redistribution assumed
   * - :math:`k_1,\ k_3`
     - 0.44, 0.54
     - Eq. (5.10a)/(5.10b) recommended values
   * - :math:`k_2,\ k_4`
     - :math:`1.25\,(0.6 + 0.0014/\varepsilon_{cu2})`
     - Recommended expression
   * - :math:`C_{Rd,c}`
     - :math:`0.18/\gamma_c`
     - §6.2.2(1) recommended value

.. warning::

   With :math:`\delta = 0.85` and the recommended :math:`k_1/k_2`, the neutral axis
   limit is :math:`x_u/d \le 0.328`. A UK National Annex user (:math:`k_1 = 0.4`,
   :math:`k_2 = 1.0`) would get 0.45 and a correspondingly higher :math:`M_{lim}`.
   This is a legitimate national choice, not an error — but it does mean mento is
   more conservative than a UK-NA calculation about when compression reinforcement
   becomes necessary.

Flexure
-------

Stress block
^^^^^^^^^^^^

§3.1.7(3) rectangular stress distribution: a uniform stress :math:`\eta f_{cd}` over
a depth :math:`\lambda x` measured from the extreme compression fibre.

.. math::

   \lambda = \begin{cases}
   0.8 & f_{ck} \le 50\ \text{MPa} \\[2pt]
   0.8 - \dfrac{f_{ck} - 50}{400} & f_{ck} > 50\ \text{MPa}
   \end{cases}
   \qquad
   \eta = \begin{cases}
   1.0 & f_{ck} \le 50\ \text{MPa} \\[2pt]
   1.0 - \dfrac{f_{ck} - 50}{200} & f_{ck} > 50\ \text{MPa}
   \end{cases}

.. important::

   Throughout mento, :math:`x_{eff}` denotes the **block depth** :math:`\lambda x`,
   never the neutral axis. The lever arm is therefore
   :math:`z = d - x_{eff}/2 = d - \lambda x/2`. Applying :math:`\lambda` a second
   time to :math:`x_{eff}` is the single easiest mistake to make here — it
   under-sizes the tension steel by about 2%. See :ref:`en-decisions`.

Ductility limit
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Both limits used by mento are written on the **neutral axis ratio**
:math:`x_u/d`: the redistribution limit of §5.5(4), Eqs. (5.10a)/(5.10b),
and an additional fixed cap of 0.45 chosen by mento. The latter is not
prescribed by §5.5(4). The 0.45/0.35 limits in §5.6.3(2) belong to the
rotation check of yielding hinges, which this helper does not implement.

.. math::

   \xi_{u,lim} = \min\left(\frac{\delta - k_1}{k_2},\ 0.45\right)
   \qquad
   x_{u,lim} = \xi_{u,lim}\, d
   \qquad
   x_{eff,lim} = \lambda\, x_{u,lim}

(:math:`k_3/k_4` replace :math:`k_1/k_2` above C50/60.) The conversion to a block
depth happens once, in ``_compression_zone_limits_EN_1992_2004``, and every caller
receives both depths already in the right units.

The limiting moment that separates singly from doubly reinforced is then

.. math::

   M_{lim} = \eta\, f_{cd}\, b\, x_{eff,lim}\left(d - \frac{x_{eff,lim}}{2}\right)

Required reinforcement — :math:`M_{Ed} \le M_{lim}`
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

No compression reinforcement is needed. Inverting
:math:`M = \eta f_{cd} b\, x_{eff}(d - x_{eff}/2)`:

.. math::

   K = \frac{M_{Ed}}{b\,d^2\,\eta\,f_{cd}}
   \qquad
   x_{eff} = d\left(1 - \sqrt{1 - 2K}\right)
   \qquad
   z = d - \frac{x_{eff}}{2}

.. math::

   A_{s1} = \max\left(\frac{M_{Ed}}{z\,f_{yd}},\ A_{s,min}\right)
   \qquad
   A_{s2} = 0

This is algebraically identical to the closed form published in *Concise Eurocode 2*
(The Concrete Centre), :math:`z/d = \left[1 + \sqrt{1 - 3.529K'}\right]/2` with
:math:`K' = M/(f_{ck} b d^2)` — a test pins the agreement.

Required reinforcement — :math:`M_{Ed} > M_{lim}`
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The concrete saturates at the ductility limit and the excess is carried by a couple
between tension and compression steel:

.. math::

   z = d - \frac{x_{eff,lim}}{2}
   \qquad
   A_{s1,lim} = \frac{M_{lim}}{z\,f_{yd}}
   \qquad
   \Delta M = M_{Ed} - M_{lim}

The compression steel stress follows from strain compatibility at :math:`x_{u,lim}`:

.. math::

   \varepsilon_{s2} = \frac{x_{u,lim} - d'}{x_{u,lim}}\,\varepsilon_{cu2}
   \qquad
   f_{sd} = \min\left(\varepsilon_{s2} E_s,\ f_{yd}\right)

.. math::

   A_{s2} = \frac{\Delta M}{(d - d')\, f_{sd}}
   \qquad
   A_{s1} = \max\left(A_{s1,lim} + A_{s2},\ A_{s,min}\right)

Minimum and maximum reinforcement
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

§9.2.1.1:

.. math::

   \rho_{min} = \max\left(0.26\,\frac{f_{ctm}}{f_{yk}},\ 0.0013\right)
   \qquad
   \rho_{max} = 0.04

with :math:`f_{ctm} = 0.30\, f_{ck}^{2/3}` (Table 3.1). Unlike ACI, :math:`\rho_{min}`
is not conditioned on the moment being non-zero, so a shear-only combination already
receives minimum flexural steel.

The minimum is written on :math:`b_t d`, as §9.2.1.1(1) writes it. The maximum of
§9.2.1.1(3) is :math:`0.04\, A_c`, and :math:`A_c` is the cross-sectional area of the
concrete (§1.6): :math:`A_{s,max} = 0.04\, b h`, the same on both faces.

The maximum applies to "tension or compression reinforcement", so unlike ACI's it
caps both faces, whatever a combination does to them, and compression steel does not
extend it. The check warns ``As_above_max`` on either face past it, and the design
never places a layout past it: when no layout within it carries the moment, it
leaves the most it may and warns ``As_below_required``.

Design bending resistance
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

For a given layout, the block depth that balances the whole tension steel is

.. math::

   x_{eff} = \frac{A_s f_{yd}}{\eta\, f_{cd}\, b}

If :math:`x_{eff} \le x_{eff,lim}` the section does not need compression steel for
equilibrium, and any bar on the compression face is a detailing or erection bar:

.. math::

   M_{Rd} = A_s f_{yd}\left(d - \frac{x_{eff}}{2}\right)

Otherwise the concrete saturates and the excess tension steel pairs with the
compression steel actually available:

.. math::

   A_{s,lim} = \frac{\eta f_{cd}\, b\, x_{eff,lim}}{f_{yd}}
   \qquad
   M_{Rd} = M_{lim} + \min\left(A_s - A_{s,lim},\ A'_s\right) f_{yd}\,(d - d')

At :math:`x_{eff} = x_{eff,lim}` the two branches coincide, so :math:`M_{Rd}` is
continuous, and it is monotonic in :math:`A'_s`. See :ref:`en-decisions` for why
that matters.

Shear
-----

Members without shear reinforcement
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

§6.2.2(1), with :math:`C_{Rd,c} = 0.18/\gamma_c` and :math:`k_1 = 0.15`:

.. math::

   V_{Rd,c} = \left[C_{Rd,c}\,k\,(100\,\rho_l\,f_{ck})^{1/3}
                    + k_1 \sigma_{cp}\right] b_w d

bounded below by the minimum of §6.2.2(2):

.. math::

   V_{Rd,c,min} = \left(v_{min} + k_1 \sigma_{cp}\right) b_w d
   \qquad
   v_{min} = 0.035\,k^{3/2}\sqrt{f_{ck}}

with

.. math::

   k = \min\left(1 + \sqrt{\frac{200}{d}},\ 2.0\right)
   \qquad
   \rho_l = \min\left(\frac{A_{sl} + A_p}{b_w d},\ 0.02\right)
   \qquad
   \sigma_{cp} = \min\left(\frac{N_{Ed}}{A_c},\ 0.2 f_{cd}\right)

(:math:`d` in mm, :math:`f_{ck}` in MPa.) The governing value is
:math:`\max(V_{Rd,c},\ V_{Rd,c,min})`. Note that every :math:`f_{cd}` in the shear
provisions uses :math:`\alpha_{cc} = 1.0`, not the 0.85 adopted for flexure.

Members with shear reinforcement
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

§6.2.3, variable strut inclination, with :math:`z = 0.9d`,
:math:`\nu_1 = 0.6\,(1 - f_{ck}/250)` and :math:`\alpha_{cw} = 1` for
non-prestressed members. The strut crushing limit is

.. math::

   V_{Rd,max} = \frac{\alpha_{cw}\, b_w\, z\, \nu_1\, f_{cd}}
                     {\cot\theta + \tan\theta}

mento selects :math:`\theta` within the code range :math:`21.8° \le \theta \le 45°`
(:math:`1 \le \cot\theta \le 2.5`), taking the flattest strut that works — which
minimises the required stirrups:

1. Evaluate :math:`V_{Rd,max}` at :math:`\theta = 21.8°`. If
   :math:`V_{Ed} \le` that value, use it.
2. Otherwise evaluate at :math:`\theta = 45°`. If :math:`V_{Ed}` exceeds even that,
   the section fails the crushing check; :math:`\theta = 45°` is kept and
   ``max_shear_ok`` is false.
3. In between, solve for the exact angle from
   :math:`\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}\arcsin\!\left(V_{Ed}/V_{Rd,max,45°}\right)`.

The stirrup contribution and the required area are then

.. math::

   V_{Rd,s} = \frac{A_{sw}}{s}\, z\, f_{ywd} \cot\theta
   \qquad
   \left(\frac{A_{sw}}{s}\right)_{req} =
       \max\left(\frac{V_{Ed}}{z\, f_{ywd} \cot\theta},\ \rho_{w,min} b_w \sin\alpha\right)

.. note::

   For a section **with** stirrups, mento reports :math:`V_{Rd} = V_{Rd,s}` — the
   concrete term is not added, per §6.2.3(2), where the truss model replaces
   :math:`V_{Rd,c}` rather than supplementing it. This differs from ACI, where
   :math:`V_c` and :math:`V_s` are summed.

Minimum reinforcement and spacing
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

§9.2.2(5):

.. math::

   \rho_{w,min} = \frac{0.08\sqrt{f_{ck}}}{f_{ywk}}
   \qquad
   \left(\frac{A_{sw}}{s}\right)_{min} = \rho_{w,min}\, b_w \sin\alpha

with :math:`\alpha` the stirrup inclination, fixed at 90° (vertical stirrups).

Spacing limits, §9.2.2(6) and (8), Expressions (9.6N) and (9.8N):

.. math::

   s_{max,l} = \min\left(0.75\,d\,(1 + \cot\alpha),\ 400\ \text{mm}\right)
   \qquad
   s_{max,w} = \min\left(0.75\,d,\ 600\ \text{mm}\right)

Expression (9.8N) carries the 600 mm cap. Expression (9.6N) has none: the 400 mm on
:math:`s_{max,l}` is mento's own, not the code's, and is kept as it is. EN 1992-1-1 has no
threshold that halves these limits, so a ``ShearCheck`` of this code reports
``V_s_req``, ``V_s_threshold`` and ``spacing_halved`` as ``None``; on a section with no
stirrups the check sets no limit, and ``s_max_l_table`` and ``s_max_w`` are ``None`` too.

The limit across the width is on the legs, spread as under ACI 318-19: ``n`` closed
stirrups put :math:`2n` legs evenly between the centres of the outermost pair,

.. math::

   s_w = \frac{b - 2c_c - d_{b,st}}{2n - 1}

in a cage of one perimeter stirrup and inner closed stirrups on the 2nd and 3rd legs,
the 4th and 5th... (indices ``(1, 2)``, ``(3, 4)`` in ``beam.section_geometry``, counting
from 0).

.. note::

   §6.2.1(4) requires minimum shear reinforcement even where
   :math:`V_{Ed} \le V_{Rd,c}`, but allows it to be omitted in members where
   transverse load redistribution is possible — slabs, and members of minor
   importance. mento reports :math:`A_{sw,req} = A_{sw,min}` in that situation and
   leaves the omission to the engineer.

.. _en-decisions:

Implementation decisions
------------------------

Lever arm: :math:`\lambda` applied exactly once
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Inverting :math:`K` yields the **block depth**, so the lever arm is
:math:`z = d - 0.5\,x_{eff}`. Writing :math:`z = d - 0.5\,\lambda\,x_{eff}` applies
:math:`\lambda` twice and gives :math:`d - 0.32x` instead of :math:`d - 0.4x`. For a
C25 200×600 section at 150 kN·m that is 518.3 mm instead of 507.9 mm — a 2%
overestimate of the lever arm, hence a 2% **under-estimate** of the required steel.

The single application is pinned by a test that recomputes the Concise Eurocode 2
closed form from scratch.

Ductility limits live on the neutral axis
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

:math:`(\delta - k_1)/k_2` and the 0.45 cap are both limits on :math:`x_u/d`. Feeding
either one straight into :math:`M_{lim}` as though it were a block depth inflates the
threshold at which compression steel becomes necessary — by about 20% for C25. The
single conversion point avoids the mix-up.

Compression steel enters only when the section needs it
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The classic decomposition
:math:`M = (A_s - A'_s) f_{yd} z_1 + A'_s f_{yd}(d - d')` is valid only when the
compression steel is genuinely required for equilibrium. Applied to an
under-reinforced section it moves the whole internal couple onto the shorter lever
arm :math:`(d - d')` and **loses** capacity as :math:`A'_s` grows — adding steel
would reduce the computed :math:`M_{Rd}`, which is physically impossible.

mento therefore keys the branch on :math:`x_{eff}` versus :math:`x_{eff,lim}`, not on
how much steel happens to sit on the compression face. Below the limit the
compression bars are ignored, which is the safe side and is also what the mechanics
say: the concrete alone balances the tension steel.

Effective depth per face
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Each face is solved with its own effective depth: the top face uses
:math:`d = h - d'_{top}` with the bottom cover as the compression side, and vice
versa. For shear, :math:`d_{shear} = \min(d_{bot},\ d_{top})`.

Supplementary web reinforcement
--------------------------------

``skin_reinforcement`` implements a limited, rectangular pure-bending
proposal under EN 1992-1-1:2004 §7.3.3(3), for h >= 1000 mm. Minimum area
uses §7.3.2(2), Eq. (7.1), with k=0.5 and sigma_s=f_yk. Taking k_c=0.4,
A_ct=b*h/2 and splitting additional area equally between side faces are
Mento's rectangular-bending interpretation, not a general axial-force rule.

The Table 7.2N diameter route uses half the independently supplied main
service stress. Table 7.3N is an alternative and is not imposed cumulatively.
The geometric substitution in Eq. (7.7N) is not specified expressly for
skin bars. Mento uses the smaller of its bending-depth and web-as-tie
interpretations; see the beams guide for both substitutions and limitations.
Table 7.2N Note 1 assumptions and the need for evenly distributed skin
under §7.3.3(3) still require engineering review.

This does not calculate crack width using §7.3.4, does not verify cracking
of the main reinforcement, and does not implement restraint/early-age
cracking, prestressing, strut-and-tie design, torsion or seismic detailing.
Axial cases explicitly report unsupported. Informative Annex J surface
mesh outside the links remains a separate project/National Annex review.
Equation/table regression tests for this feature must not be mistaken for
independent published beam validation or a direct calculated w_k check.
